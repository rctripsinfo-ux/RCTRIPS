# Script (approved)

Facts: Florence, Oregon, 12 Nov 1970. 45-ft, ~8-ton sperm whale. Oregon Highway Division used 20 cases (~half a ton) of
dynamite. Walter Umenhofer (ex-military, explosives training) said ~20 sticks would do. Blubber rained on spectators
and the parking lot; a large piece crushed a car roof; the state reimbursed Umenhofer for his car. Nobody was hurt.
The seagulls were scared off; the remains were buried. Sources: Wikipedia "Exploding whale", Popular Science,
Mental Floss, Amusing Planet.

Oregon once tried to make a dead whale disappear. Instead, they made it rain whale. November 1970. A forty-five-foot
sperm whale washes up on the beach at Florence. Eight tons. And it stinks. Bury it? The tide would dig it right back up.
Tow it? Way too heavy. So the highway department has a better idea: blow it up. Tiny pieces, and the seagulls will clean
up the rest. One man in the crowd, Walter Umenhofer, learned explosives in the army. He told them twenty sticks would do
it. They brought twenty cases. Half a ton of dynamite. Crowds gather. Cameras roll. Three. Two. One. For one second, it's
beautiful. Then the sky starts falling. Chunks of blubber rain on the crowd, on the dunes, all the way to the parking
lot. Luckily, nobody was hurt. Unluckily, it was still raining whale. You see, a blast always goes where it's easiest.
Step on a ketchup packet: the ketchup doesn't go into the floor. It shoots out sideways, straight at your friend. The
packed sand wouldn't move, so all that force went the easy way. Up. And toward the people. And the seagulls? The boom
scared every single one away. Most of the whale was still lying there. So the crew buried it anyway. And the one man who
said, too much dynamite? The biggest chunk landed right on his car.

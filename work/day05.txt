You start your squats feeling fine, but by the tenth rep your knees begin to ache. You try going slower and lighter, and they still feel sore. If that sounds like you, the problem might not be your knees at all.

Your glutes are the big muscles in your butt. Part of their job is to keep your knees steady when you bend down. When they're weak, your knees often cave in toward each other as you squat, and that puts extra stress on them.

Here are three easy moves to fix it. First, glute bridges. Lie on your back with your knees bent, then squeeze your butt and lift your hips up. Do fifteen. Second, clamshells. Lie on your side with your knees bent and open your top knee like a book. Do fifteen on each side. Third, box squats. Sit back onto a chair, push your knees out so they stay in line with your toes, and stand up. Do ten.

Do these three times a week, and watch your knees in the mirror when you squat. If the pain is sharp or doesn't go away, see a doctor.

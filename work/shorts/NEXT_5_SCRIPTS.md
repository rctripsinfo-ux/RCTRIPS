## 4. Hanoi paid people to kill rats, and got more rats (1902)
**Thumbnail (frame 0):** a shocked French official holding a basket of rat tails; behind him, tail-less rats running out of a sewer.
**Script:**
In 1902, Hanoi paid people to kill rats, and ended up with more rats.
Hanoi is ruled by France. The French build a brand-new sewer system, the pride of their modern city.
The rats love it. Warm, dark tunnels, straight into the rich neighborhoods' toilets.
So officials hire rat catchers. They kill twenty thousand rats in a single day. (verify) Still not enough.
New plan: anyone can earn one cent per rat. Just bring the tail as proof.
Tails pour in. Thousands of them.
Luckily, the city looks cleaner. Unluckily, officials start seeing rats in the streets... with no tails.
You see, a rat without a tail can still have babies. People cut off the tail, let the rat go, and wait for the next payday. It's like paying someone for every apple, and they plant an orchard.
Then inspectors find farms outside the city, breeding rats. On purpose.
The reward is cancelled. The rats stay.
And the twist? The sewers built to make Hanoi modern were the rats' highway the whole time.
**Story plan:** colonial boulevard + sewer cut-away; rat catchers climbing out of manholes; a desk with a mountain of tails;
tail-less rats in the street (CU); night explanation: rat family growing; a hidden rat farm in a shed; wide final of the sewer map glowing as a "highway".

## 5. NASA lost a spacecraft because two teams used different units (1999)
**Thumbnail:** a shocked engineer at a mission-control desk, Mars and a tiny orbiter on the big screen behind him.
**Script:**
NASA lost a 125-million-dollar spacecraft because two teams used different units.
September 1999. The Mars Climate Orbiter has flown for nine months, more than six hundred million kilometers. Tonight it slides into orbit around Mars.
The engine fires. The ship disappears behind the planet. Everyone waits for its signal to come back.
It never does.
Luckily, the investigators find the cause in weeks. Unluckily, it's embarrassing.
You see, one team's software measured the push of the small thrusters in pounds of force. The navigation team read those numbers as newtons. One pound is about four and a half newtons. So every push was four and a half times bigger than the computer thought. It's like a recipe that says two cups, and you use two buckets.
For months, the tiny errors added up. The orbiter should have arrived about a hundred and fifty kilometers above Mars. It arrived at fifty-seven. Far too low.
And the contract had said: use metric.
And the twist? The orbiter was built to study the weather on Mars. It probably burned up in it.
**Story plan:** 1999 mission control (beige computers, coffee cups); the orbiter in space; Mars limb + engine burn; the
signal bar staying empty; night explanation: two engineers with a pound weight and a newton apple on a see-saw; kitchen gag
(cups vs buckets); trajectory line dipping into the glowing atmosphere; final: the engineer with the contract stamped METRIC (no text: a big ruler icon).

## 6. The 1904 Olympic marathon was almost won by a man in a car
**Thumbnail:** a grinning runner waving from the back seat of an old car, dusty road, a shocked official behind.
**Script:**
The 1904 Olympic marathon was almost won by a man who rode in a car.
St. Louis, August. Over thirty degrees. Dirt roads. Cars kicking up dust. And only one water stop in forty kilometers. On purpose. The organizer wanted to study thirst.
Fred Lorz gives up after about fifteen kilometers and hops into a car. The car breaks down, so he jogs the rest of the way into the stadium. The crowd cheers. He's about to get the gold medal... until someone reveals he rode in a car.
Meanwhile, Thomas Hicks is collapsing. His trainers give him egg whites, brandy, and a little strychnine. Rat poison. Back then, it was used to keep tired muscles going.
Luckily, he wins. Unluckily, he can barely stand, and his trainers have to hold him up across the line.
You see, without water your body can't sweat, and the heat builds up inside you like a car parked in the sun.
A Cuban mailman ran in street clothes, ate rotten apples from an orchard, took a nap, and still finished fourth.
Only fourteen of thirty-two runners finished.
And the twist? The man who rode in the car was banned. The next year, he won the Boston Marathon.
**Story plan:** dusty country road with spectators and 1904 cars; Lorz waving from a car (CU); the stadium finish with
the medal; Hicks with trainers and an egg; night explanation: a car in the sun with a thermometer; the mailman napping under an apple tree; the Boston finish line twist.

## 7. A city cured a dancing plague by hiring a band (Strasbourg, 1518)
**Thumbnail:** a shocked doctor in a beaked hat (or robe) on a wooden stage, crowds of dancers behind him.
**Script:**
In 1518, a city tried to cure a dancing sickness... by hiring a band.
Strasbourg, July. A woman named Frau Troffea steps into the street and starts dancing. No music. She can't stop. Hours. Days.
A week later, dozens are dancing with her. By August, hundreds. (verify numbers)
The doctors decide it's hot blood. And the cure for hot blood? Dance it out.
So the city clears two halls, builds a wooden stage, hires musicians, and pays strong men to hold the dancers up.
Luckily, the dancers get music. Unluckily, more people join in.
You see, it was probably a mass panic, not a disease. The city had famine, sickness, and a belief that a saint could curse you to dance. When you're scared and everyone around you is doing it, your body copies them. Like a yawn going around a room.
So the city flips the plan. Music is banned. Public dancing is banned. The dancers are taken to a shrine in the hills, given red shoes and prayers.
By September, it stops.
And the twist? The cure for the dancing plague turned out to be... no dancing.
**Story plan:** medieval square with timber houses; one woman dancing alone; the square filling with dancers; doctors
with a big book; carpenters building the stage, musicians playing, strong men holding dancers; night explanation: a yawn spreading along a row of faces; the shrine in the hills with red shoes; final: silent square, a "no music" pictogram.

## 8. Spain ordered trains too big for its own tunnels (2020-2023)
**Thumbnail:** a shocked engineer holding a measuring tape between a shiny new train and a tunnel that's clearly too small.
**Script:**
Spain ordered thirty-one new trains that were too big for its own tunnels.
Northern Spain, 2020. The mountain railways of Cantabria and Asturias are old. So the train company orders thirty-one shiny new trains. Price: two hundred and fifty-eight million euros.
The designers get the measurements and start drawing.
Then, a year later, the builder compares the plans with the old mountain tunnels... and the numbers don't match. The new trains won't fit.
Luckily, nobody had built them yet. Unluckily, it took a year to notice.
You see, many of those tunnels were dug more than a hundred years ago, and not all of them are the same size. The plans used the wrong measurements. It's like buying a sofa that fits a normal door... and your door is from 1890.
When the news comes out in 2023, the head of the train company and a top transport official resign.
New trains are ordered. Delivery: years later.
And the twist? The old trains they wanted to replace are still running, and they still fit.
**Story plan:** green mountain valley with an old stone tunnel; a cheerful office signing the order; designers drawing a sleek
train; the builder holding the plan against the tunnel mouth (gap visible); the sofa-and-old-door gag at night; a press
conference with empty chairs; final: the old train chugging through the tunnel, a tiny gap on each side.

H: One hundred push-ups. Every day. For thirty days. No gym, no weights, no days off.
M: That's three thousand push-ups! You can barely do twenty!
H: Exactly. So let's see what actually happens to the body. Day by day.
M: And if it goes wrong, I get to say I told you so.
H: Day one. First, a test. Max push-ups in one go, clean form. Chest close to the floor, body straight like a plank.
H: Twenty-two. That's where we start.
M: So how do you get to a hundred?
H: You don't do them all at once. You split them. Five sets of twenty, or ten sets of ten, spread across the day.
M: So, push-ups before breakfast. Push-ups before lunch. Push-ups before you cry.
H: Pretty much. Day one, a hundred done. It felt easy.
M: Famous last words.
H: Day two. Chest sore. Arms sore. Lifting a cup of tea feels like a workout.
M: Is that the muscle growing?
H: No. That's normal muscle soreness from a new exercise. It peaks around one or two days after, then it fades.
H: Soreness is not a sign of growth. It's a sign of something new.
M: So the pain is just... a welcome message?
H: A loud one. Day three, I did the hundred anyway, but in smaller sets. Ten at a time. Form first.
H: End of week one, the muscles feel better. But something else starts talking. The wrists.
M: Your wrists have opinions now?
H: Seven hundred push-ups in a week is a lot of pressure on bent wrists.
H: Simple fix: do them on your fists, or hold two dumbbells, so the wrist stays straight. And spread your fingers wide.
M: Fist push-ups. Very action movie.
H: Also, elbows. Don't flare them out like a T. Keep them at about forty-five degrees from your body. Your shoulders will thank you.
H: Week two, something strange happens. The hundred gets faster. The sets get bigger. Twenty, twenty-five in a row.
M: Wait, did you grow muscle that fast?
H: Not really. Most of the early strength comes from the brain and the nerves, not new muscle.
H: Your nervous system learns the movement. It switches on more muscle fibres, at the same time, more smoothly.
M: So your muscles didn't get bigger. They just got... organised.
H: Exactly. Same team, better coordination. Muscle growth you can actually see usually takes more like six to eight weeks.
H: Week three. The mirror.
M: Ooh. Abs? Huge chest? Movie star?
H: The chest and the back of the arms look a little fuller. Mostly a pump, and a little real growth.
H: Posture looks better too, because a push-up is basically a moving plank. The core works the whole time.
M: And the belly?
H: Same belly. A hundred push-ups burn maybe thirty to fifty calories. That's one biscuit.
M: One biscuit?! Three weeks of pain for a biscuit?
H: Push-ups build strength. Fat loss comes from food and walking. Different jobs.
H: Week four. The hundred is now easy. Four sets of twenty-five. Done in ten minutes.
M: That's good, right?
H: It's good, but it's also the problem. When something gets easy, the body stops changing. It has no reason to.
M: So the challenge... stops being a challenge.
H: Right. Muscles grow when you keep asking for a little more. More reps, a harder version, or slower reps.
H: Day thirty. The final test. Max push-ups in one go.
M: Drumroll please...
H: Thirty-eight. From twenty-two to thirty-eight in one month.
M: That's almost double!
H: Here's the honest summary. Strength: way up. Endurance: way up. Chest and arms: a little bigger. Belly fat: no change. Wrists: survived, after the fix.
M: So should people do it or not?
H: Do it. But do the smarter version. Three rules.
H: Rule one. Split the reps across the day. Never grind them out in one ugly set.
H: Rule two. Take one easy day a week. Fifty push-ups instead of a hundred. Joints need recovery too.
H: Rule three. After two weeks, make it harder. Slow the way down to three seconds, or raise your feet on a chair.
M: And if you can't do one push-up?
H: Then start on your knees, or with your hands on a wall or a table. Same muscles, less weight. A hundred wall push-ups still counts.
M: Okay. Who's actually starting tomorrow?
H: Do the day one test. Comment your max push-ups right now. And come back in thirty days with your new number.
M: No lying. We will know.

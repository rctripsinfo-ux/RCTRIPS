# LONG 06: "How Skinny Guys Beat Big Guys (It's Not Muscle)"  16:9, target ~6 min, STORY format
# Reference topic: "How Some Skinny Guys Are Stronger Than Big Guys" (The Rep Theory, 1.1M in 1 month)
# Characters: H (big, narrator, first person "I") and KABIR (new: a lean, wiry, calm guy in a grey tank top). Mini-H: only 2 short lines.
# New look vs older videos: a story with one location per chapter (gym arm-wrestling table, H's apartment at night, a rock-climbing wall,
# a construction site, a garage gym, the rematch), "CLUE #N" case-file cards on dark navy (not red mistake cards), X-ray / nerve views,
# slow-motion moments. Open loop: the rematch result is only shown at the end.
# ">" lines = beat sheet (what is on screen), not spoken.

## THE LOSS
> gym, arm-wrestling table, crowd silhouettes; H flexes, Kabir sits calm, sleeves of a grey tank top
H: I weigh ninety kilos. I train six days a week. And last month, I lost an arm-wrestling match to a guy who weighs sixty.
> slow motion: H's hand slams to the table, H's face shocked; Kabir doesn't even sweat
H: His name is Kabir. Skinny arms. No pump. No supplements. He beat me in four seconds.
> stopwatch 0:04
H: So I asked him to do it again. He beat me again. Then he did twenty pull-ups like it was nothing.
> Kabir pull-up loop fast, counter to 20; H watching, jaw dropped
H: I couldn't sleep that night. How is a guy with half my muscle stronger than me?
> H at night in bed, eyes open, ceiling
H: The answer is not muscle. It's three things almost nobody trains. And by the end of this video, I'll show you what happened in the rematch.

## CLUE 1: THE WIRES
> card CLUE #1 / THE WIRES
H: Here's the first thing I learned. A muscle doesn't move by itself. Your brain sends a signal down your nerves, and that signal decides how much of the muscle actually fires.
> X-ray view: brain glows, a lightning line runs down the arm, muscle fibres light up
H: Every muscle is made of thousands of little units. A beginner can only switch on part of them at the same time. The rest just sit there.
> fibres: some light up, many stay grey | "PART OF IT FIRES"
H: Years of heavy lifting teach your nervous system to switch on more of them, faster, all at once.
> fibres light up all together, bright | "MORE. FASTER. TOGETHER."
H: Kabir's muscles are smaller. But when he pulls, almost all of it fires. Mine had more muscle and a worse connection.
> side by side: big arm with half the fibres lit vs thin arm fully lit
M: So you bought a sports car and never learned to drive.
H: Exactly.

## CLUE 2: THE TRAINING
> card CLUE #2 / THE TRAINING
H: So I asked Kabir how he trains. His answer made me feel stupid.
> garage gym, Kabir lifting heavy, few reps
H: I train like a bodybuilder. Ten to fifteen reps, chase the burn, chase the pump.
> H pumping curls, sweat, mirror | "10-15 REPS = SIZE"
H: Kabir does the opposite. Heavy weight. Three to five reps. Long rest. He never chases the burn.
> Kabir heavy low reps, then sits calm with a stopwatch 3:00 | "3-5 REPS = STRENGTH"
H: High reps mostly build size. Heavy low reps mostly build strength. Both build muscle, but only one teaches your body to lift something really heavy.
> two bars: SIZE vs STRENGTH
H: Strength is a skill. You get good at what you practice. I practiced getting a pump. He practiced being strong.

## CLUE 3: THE CABLES
> card CLUE #3 / THE CABLES
H: The third clue was on his hands. His fingers were thick. His forearms looked like cables.
> close-up of Kabir's hands and forearms, veins
H: Kabir grew up climbing rocks and carrying bricks on his uncle's construction site.
> flashback: young Kabir climbing a rock wall; carrying bricks
H: Years of that made his tendons strong. Tendons are the cables that connect muscle to bone. Stiffer cables pass force faster, with less lost on the way.
> X-ray: muscle, tendon (white cable), bone; force arrow travels along the tendon
H: Muscle grows in weeks. Tendons take months and years. That's why the strongest grip in the gym often belongs to the guy who looks the least impressive.

## THE GENETIC CARD
H: And there's one thing neither of us can change. Leverage.
> two arms side by side with joint pivot points and lever lines
H: Shorter forearms and tendons that attach a little further from the joint give some people a natural advantage, like a longer handle on a wrench.
> wrench with short handle vs long handle, the long one turns easier
H: Kabir has short forearms. In arm wrestling, that's a cheat code. You can't train that. But the first three, you can.

## THE PLAN
> card THE PLAN / 8 WEEKS
H: So I made a plan. Eight weeks. Three changes.
H: One. Heavy and low. Three days a week, three to five reps on the big lifts, with three minutes of rest.
> H heavy rows / heavy push-ups with a weight vest, rest timer
H: Two. Grip every day. Dead hangs, towel hangs, farmer carries.
> dead hang loop, towel hang, farmer carry walk
H: Three. Practice the actual skill. Twice a week, I arm-wrestled anyone who would say yes.
> H arm-wrestling different people, losing, then winning
M: You lost to the gym's receptionist.
H: Twice. We don't talk about it.

## THE REMATCH
> card THE REMATCH
H: Eight weeks later. Same table. Same guy.
> gym table, crowd, slow push-in, both lock hands, heartbeat
H: He went first. I didn't move. Five seconds. Ten seconds.
> slow motion, hands shaking in the middle, timer counting
H: Fifteen seconds in, I felt it. Everything fired at once.
> X-ray of H's arm: all fibres light up together
H: And I won.
> slam, H's fist up, Kabir smiling and shaking his hand
H: Then Kabir pointed at the pull-up bar and said: best of three.
> Kabir at the bar smiling; H's face drops
H: I lost that one. I'm still not a fan of pull-ups.

## THE LESSON
H: Here's what I learned. Muscle is the engine. Your nerves are the driver, your tendons are the cables, and your training decides which one you build.
> engine + driver + cables icons appear one by one around H
H: If you want to look strong, chase the pump. If you want to be strong, lift heavy, train your grip, and practice the skill.
> H and Kabir side by side, both fist bump
H: Tell me in the comments: have you ever lost to a skinny guy? Be honest.
